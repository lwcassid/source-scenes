## Two numbers

Stand at the pedestal, raise your hands. Two beams read where they are and
hand the wall two numbers — left and right, 0 to 1. Every scene in this
library is an answer to one question: **what can light and sound do with
only those two numbers?**

You call it a visual theremin. We call it the source. Same instrument.

## What a scene has to do

A stranger walks up, moves a hand, and the room answers — light and sound
— fast enough that they *know* it was them. That's the whole test. The
rest follows:

- **One second.** A stranger knows what their hands do within a second.
  Adding a second mechanic has killed more versions than anything else.
- **Nothing waits.** The hand moves, the whole picture moves, now. Beats
  and events snap to the grid; the continuous response never does.
- **Full frame.** The picture is one field and the hand changes what it's
  made of. Small objects and unlocks read as screensavers.
- **Black is invisible.** It's projected on mesh. Light is a spend; thin
  lines vanish; perspective shatters across hanging drapes.
- **It sounds finished with nobody touching it.** Then the hands add
  expression. The mids stay empty for the band.

## How it's built

A scene is one JavaScript file with four functions: `init` (make the
world), `step` (the hands move it), `draw` (paint it), `audio` (sing it).
No engine, no framework — the site is those files concatenated. Push to
`main`, it's live in a minute.

Sound is three layers: a **drone** (a pedal with real chord colour), a
**grid** (chord changes, and drums only when earned), and a **reactive**
layer (the gesture, now). The browser plays a sketch of it; the finished
sound is **Ableton** — every note is mirrored as MIDI on a role (pad, bass,
lead, bells, perc) and a real rack answers.

**Nothing is ever overwritten.** Every round becomes a new version — V1,
V2, V3 — and every old one stays in the dropdown, playable. Break nothing,
ever.

## How you work

You don't install anything and you don't code. Go to claude.ai/code, pick
`source-scenes`, say who you are, and talk:

> "Show me Orbits as it is right now."
> "New version: slower stretch, the far ends glow longer."
> "Transmission's kit is too busy — kick and sub only."

It builds the version, looks at screenshots of its own work in the
projector frame, iterates, verifies the wall still runs, and publishes.
For sound, it'll pitch the idea before writing a note — say yes or bend
it; that step saves rounds.

## Bringing your own work

Don't port files. Bring the **idea**: what the picture is made of, and
what your hands do to it. A screen recording or a few stills help. Claude
rebuilds it as a scene in our software, and it gains everything the wall
gives for free — the harmony engine, MIDI to the rack, the projector
frame, versions, the queue. Then you play it and push back. The wall's
physics still apply: black invisible, fat marks, radial and field
compositions over horizons.

## The show

Tick scenes into the **queue**; its order is the running order, each with
minutes on stage. Saved sets open with one link — yours is `#set=TEMPLE`.
**PLAY** runs the pre-flight and walks the order fullscreen.

## Three rules

Work on your own scenes. Say in the chat when you're publishing. When you
give a verdict — "never again", "that's the reference" — it gets written
into the notebook that session, or it didn't happen.
