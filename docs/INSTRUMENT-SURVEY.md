# INSTRUMENT SURVEY v2 — every scene scored as a playable instrument on fabric
*Revised criteria per Lance: these are visual+sound instruments first. They must be satisfying as fuck to play, and jammable with live instrumentalists. Scrim is one factor, not the frame.*

## The five criteria (scored 1–5)
**IMM · Immediacy** — a gesture makes sound NOW. Continuous coupling beats triggering beats modulating a running pattern.
**EXP · Expressive range** — how much musical space between minimal and maximal play; do the two hands mean different things; is there pitch/density/timbre to command?
**JAM · Jam-ability** — locked to key and grid; leaves rhythmic and frequency room for a live player; predictable enough to play *with* (telegraphed beats, stable voices); clean MIDI roles into Ableton.
**SAT · Satisfaction / skill curve** — juicy audiovisual payoff per action, plus something to actually get better at (aim, timing, phrasing, restraint).
**SCR · Scrim** — the fabric criteria from pass one, compressed (black field, fat marks, no perspective, slices well).

## LANCE'S TOP 3 — chosen for instrument quality, scrim, and maximum diversity between them
Three scenes, three different verbs, three roles in a band: **STRIKE, STRUM, SUSTAIN.**

### 1 · STORM GARDEN — the percussion / the spectacle (STRIKE)
Already jam-native: charge pulses are grid-locked and visibly travel one beat before landing, so a live drummer or the crowd can *anticipate*. The fix list:
- Beams 3× wider, soft-edged; clouds bigger, fewer, glowier; the whole charge state visibly swells.
- Lightning floods the entire frame (2 frames white-violet) — on the fabric volume that's a physical event.
- Sound: near-silence between strikes; charge = rising filtered tension that tracks hold-time; strike = real thunder sample + 808 sub drop on ch11; cloud-notes stay sparse bells. Silence is what makes thunder work.
- Jam hook: strikes quantize to the next half-bar, so a guitarist can lean into the landing.

### 2 · BUBBLE FIELD, absorbing CABLE STRUM — the harp (STRUM)
Your two rope scenes are one instrument split in half: Bubble Field has the world (bubbles, glissando pitch-space), Cable Strum has the string physics (tension=pitch, velocity=trigger, Karplus voice). Merge them:
- The rope becomes a chain of fat glowing beads (mesh-proof), strung across the full frame.
- Slow moves herd and RETUNE (tension bends the chord voicing); fast moves strum — physical glissando across two octaves, velocity → pluck hardness.
- Bubbles that drift onto the chain sit there as extra voices until strummed off — you can *load* the string, then rake it. That's the skill curve: build a voicing, spend it.
- Sound: Karplus pluck (Live: Tension/Corpus), dotted-8th ping-pong, every pitch on the chord ladder. The most guitarist-legible scene in the library — call and response is obvious.

### 3 · AURORA VEILS (adopt — unclaimed) — the harmonic floor (SUSTAIN)
The deliberate opposite of the other two: no events, no grid, just a choral wall whose brightness and shimmer follow the solar wind of your hands. Scrim-native today (curtains on literal veils), and the explicit *jam-with-a-live-instrumentalist* scene — it holds harmony and touches no rhythm, leaving the entire groove to the human. Fixes are light:
- Voice-led choir/strings pad (5 detuned voices, 4s attacks) + sub root; CC74 → master brightness so the sky IS the filter.
- Asymmetry payoff: dragging the storm across the volume slowly rebalances the chord voicing L→R (hard-panned).
- One rare event: both hands held high for 8 bars ignites a full-sky surge + a single sub-octave bloom. Earned, not spammed.
*Alternate third if you want drama over drone: The Rift — the only scene where two strangers must physically cooperate. Different kind of magic.*

## Killer ideas the library is missing
**GHOST LOOM — the live looper.** Nothing in the library has MEMORY. A scene where the phrase you just played — pitch and rhythm from your hand path — becomes a glowing ribbon that orbits and REPLAYS on the next bar, layerable 3–4 deep. One person becomes an ensemble; with a live musician it's structured call-and-response. The shell already has ghosts and a transport; this is the biggest instrument gap and the best jam feature we could add.
**PHASE COURT — the Reich machine.** Two arrays of pendulum lights; L and R set the two rates. Clicks pan with position; when rates are near-equal the pattern slowly phases like *Piano Phase* — hypnotic on layered drapes, pure physics, tempo-locked, and teaches polyrhythm by feel.
**VOX — the missing human voice.** Hands = vowel (formant morph a→o→u) and pitch of a spectral choir column of light. The fabric sings. Technically simple (two formant bandpasses on a saw), emotionally the biggest register the library doesn't touch.

## Note on the criteria change
Pass one optimized for the surface; this pass optimizes for the *point* — but notice the intersection is small. Only a handful of scenes are strong on BOTH axes (Storm Garden and the merged strum-harp are the standouts). That's the argument for the top-3 above: they're the rare scenes where the fabric and the instrument reinforce each other instead of trading off.

**Diversity check against the existing set list.** These add four verbs the library did not have: BURST (36), GEAR (37/40), OPEN (38), and MERGE (41/42). Sonically they spread across pops/bells, a stepped arp, a percussion-free organ, a lane melody with an earned groove, a phasing choir, converging glides, and a bowed loom — no two of them compete for the same hole in a mix. All seven are pedal-based (root never moves, colour shifts over it), so any of them can sit under a live player without a key change.

**Where they need watching.** 36 and 41 both fill the frame with circles — do not put them back to back in a rotation. 38 is the quietest thing in the library and belongs in the ambient slot (after the spectacle, before the next build). 40's slowest gear is genuinely slow: it is a three-minute scene, not a ten-second one.

## SRC-43 Cell Front — the pour, done as colour instead of shape
*Built after Foam Bloom's premise was called dead. Foam Bloom and Pour Cells both
took the SHAPE from an acrylic pour (packed round cells) and left the colour behind —
one is monochrome crimson, the other a tuned palette on discrete domes. The thing a
real pour actually does, and neither of them does, is MIX AT CELL RESOLUTION: the
heavy top film splits, each cell shows the colour that was under it, flat and walled
off by a near-black contour, so two paints meeting make a mosaic and never a gradient.
Palette sampled off the reference (an Etsy dutch pour, "Pegasus' Wing"): marigold
#ffa71c · gold #fece1b · orange #fe7e22 · cyan #4bbefc · indigo #547af4 ·
violet #822ba5 · magenta #fd1b7c · crimson #fa1b52 · cream #d8c0a4 over graphite.*

| Scene | Own | IMM | EXP | JAM | SAT | SCR | Reading |
|---|---|--|--|--|--|--|---|
| SRC-43 Cell Front | N | **5** | **5** | 4 | 4 | 4 | One hand per bank of paint. The front between them only EXISTS when both hands are out — the middle pool's size is whichever hand is doing less — so this is the first scene in the set where the two hands are not two independent channels but one shared object. Colour is pitch: deep in a bank the ladder is low and woody, at the front it is the top of it and glass, so sweeping the seam sweeps the melody and you can see the note coming. |

**Implementation note worth keeping.** The colour is looked up at the winning Voronoi
SITE, not at the pixel — one line, and it is the whole difference between a pour and a
gradient. Cell scale is driven off distance to the front (coarse in the deep banks,
shattered at the seam), and ~5% of cells are ROGUE: they take the mirrored band's
colour, which is what stops each territory reading as one flat hue.

**Where it needs watching.** It shares the packed-cells frame with 36 and 41 — three
scenes of round cells is too many for one rotation, pick one. The seam can blow out if
the lacing term is ever pushed: it is currently the only white in the scene and it
should stay a filigree, not a flare.
